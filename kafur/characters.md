# The Right Hand: Characters

The principals follow the character sketch in `Notes/18 Character Sketch`. Each **Notes** field separates what the record says from what the novel invents. Every dated fact here was checked against modern summaries of the chronicles; check it against the chronicles before relying on it.

## Principals

### Malik Kafur

*Mid-thirties (invented) • Siri, Delhi*

- **Role in Story:** the narrator. He dictates this book in the 35 days of his regency.
- **Goal:**
  - To hold the throne for the boy, and so for himself.
  - Underneath, to be known by one person as he was, before the city's version of him wins.
- **Physical Description:**
  - Beardless in a court of beards.
  - A voice that never broke.
  - Small hands.
  - Once, a beauty worth a thousand dinars; now, a thickening general's body he treats as equipment.
  - Wears the Sultan's carnelian seal on his thumb.
- **Personality:**
  - Counts everything: elephants, amirs at a grave, the cost of each oath.
  - Dry, exact and unsentimental, with a wit that surprises people.
  - Patient in the way of a man who has waited for others all his life.
  - Capable of great cruelty delivered in a level voice.
- **Occupation:**
  - *Malik Na'ib*: the Sultan's deputy, now regent for the boy Umar.
  - Formerly *barbeg*, master of the court's ceremonies.
  - Commander of the southern campaigns.
  - Governor of Devagiri.
- **Habits/Mannerisms:**
  - Touches the iron pillar whenever he passes it.
  - Stands at the right hand of whoever holds the throne.
  - Never raises his voice.
  - Leaves the room when feeling threatens to show.
- **Background:** see the record below. The novel makes him a Gujarati child of Khambhat who remembers his mother and the Gujarati months, and has lost the reckoning of both.
- **Internal Conflicts:**
  - Love for the Sultan against the knowledge of what the Sultan was.
  - A convert born Hindu who stripped the temples of the south.
  - A slave who holds all power and no standing.
- **External Conflicts:**
  - The amirs, who despise him.
  - The Malika-i-Jahan, who has hated him since 1299.
  - Mubarak Khan.
  - The paiks, whom he trusts.
- **Notes:**
  - **Record:**
    - Also called Taj al-Din Izz al-Dawla.
    - A eunuch slave in the house of a rich *khwaja* of Khambhat, who is said to have paid 1,000 dinars for him; hence *Hazar-dinari*, the Thousand-Dinar Man.
    - Nusrat Khan took him in the sack of Khambhat in 1299, had him converted, and presented him to Alauddin.
    - Isami says the Sultan favoured him because his counsel always proved fit for the occasion; Barani says the Sultan was infatuated with him.
    - As barbeg, he commanded against the Mongols on the Ravi in 1306.
    - He led the campaigns against Devagiri (1308), Warangal (1310), and the Hoysala and Pandya lands (1311).
    - In 1313 he annexed Devagiri and governed it until he was recalled in 1315.
    - After Alauddin's death he was regent for about 35 days, and was killed by Alauddin's paiks in early February 1316. He was buried in an unmarked grave.
  - **Unknown, so invented:**
    - his birth name, which the novel never gives;
    - his family;
    - his age;
    - how and when he was castrated;
    - his inner life.

### Alauddin Khilji

*About 50 at death • Siri*

- **Role in Story:**
  - The dead man at the centre of the book, alive in every Then chapter.
  - Kafur's master, maker and, in some sense the novel leaves open, his beloved.
- **Goal:** power that cannot be taken from him the way he took it: from inside the family.
- **Physical Description:**
  - The shoulders of a wrestler and a neck like a bullock's; a man a horse seemed built around.
  - At the end, wasted, with water in his legs and belly.
  - An arrow scar on the forearm from Tilpat.
- **Personality:**
  - Illiterate by Barani's account, and intelligent without any learning.
  - Suspicious, and generous as a tactic.
  - Capable of laughter, and of ordering families killed for their men's crimes.
  - Renounced wine.
- **Occupation:** Sultan of Delhi, 1296-1316.
- **Habits/Mannerisms:**
  - Asks the price of things.
  - Complains of the weight of his seal ring.
  - Tests men with questions he already knows the answer to.
- **Background:**
  - Raised by his uncle Jalaluddin after his father's death, and married to Jalaluddin's daughter, the Malika-i-Jahan.
  - Governor of Kara. His raid on Devagiri in 1296 paid for his uncle's murder and his own throne.
- **Internal Conflicts:** he knows the throne is always taken by the nearest hand, and he keeps the nearest hands close anyway.
- **External Conflicts:**
  - the Mongols;
  - his nephews;
  - his sons;
  - in the end, his own body.
- **Notes:**
  - **Record:**
    - Kara in July 1296.
    - Kili, 1299.
    - Tilpat, Ranthambore and the nephews, 1301.
    - Chittor and the Mongol siege, 1303.
    - The markets.
    - The southern campaigns.
    - The 1311 massacre of the New Muslims.
    - Died 4 January 1316, and was buried in his madrasa in front of the Jami mosque at the Qutb.
  - **Uncertain:**
    - The name of his final illness. The symptoms in chapter 1 are invented.
    - Whether Kafur hastened his death, which Barani reports.

### Shihabuddin Umar

*Six • Siri*

- **Role in Story:** the "you" of the book. Everything is dictated for him to read when he is grown.
- **Goal:** his mother, sweets, and his brother Mubarak's pigeons.
- **Physical Description:** a small boy in a brocade coat he keeps trying to take off, under a turban pinned to fit a man.
- **Personality:** laughs at the wrong things, falls asleep on the throne, and asks whether the grave is cold.
- **Occupation:** Sultan of Delhi, 5 January to 14 April 1316.
- **Background:**
  - Son of Alauddin and Jhatyapali, daughter of Ramachandra of Devagiri.
  - Born in 1310 or 1311.
- **Internal Conflicts:** none yet. That is the tragedy.
- **External Conflicts:** everyone.
- **Notes:**
  - **Record:**
    - Enthroned by Kafur at six.
    - After Kafur's death, his half-brother Mubarak blinded him and sent him to Gwalior.
    - In 1318 Mubarak had him killed there, with the brothers Kafur had blinded.
  - He never read this book. The Historical Note says so, and says nothing else.

### Jhatyapali

*Age uncertain • the women's rooms at Siri*

- **Role in Story:**
  - The boy's mother.
  - Kafur's wife of convenience, his only equal in the palace, and his most dangerous ally.
- **Goal:** to keep her son alive, whoever has to be sacrificed.
- **Physical Description:** to be decided in drafting.
- **Personality:**
  - Grew up in a palace where people knew what a throne costs, and has learned Delhi's prices.
  - Speaks in numbers when she wants to wound him ("That is not a number, Malik").
- **Occupation:** Sultan's widow; the Sultan's mother.
- **Background:**
  - Daughter of Ramachandra, the Yadava king of Devagiri.
  - Came to Delhi with her father's family in Kafur's train in 1308, on Isami's account. Some historians put her marriage to Alauddin after the 1296 raid instead.
- **Internal Conflicts:** her father's daughter against her son's mother; Devagiri against Delhi.
- **External Conflicts:**
  - the Malika-i-Jahan;
  - Mubarak;
  - Kafur, whom she needs.
- **Notes:**
  - **Record:** only her parentage, her son and her marriage to Alauddin are known.
  - **Uncertain:** her marriage to Kafur comes from Firishta alone.
  - **Invented:** everything she says.

### Qutbuddin Mubarak Khan

*About seventeen • confined in his rooms at Siri*

- **Role in Story:**
  - The third son, whom Kafur underestimates. "The third keeps pigeons."
  - The hand behind the paiks.
- **Goal:** to live, and then to rule.
- **Physical Description:**
  - A young man with his father's face and none of his father's weight.
  - Pretty; he knows it.
- **Personality:**
  - Charming, idle, frightened, and far cleverer than he lets anyone see.
  - As Sultan he became notorious, in Barani, for frivolity and cruelty.
- **Occupation:** prince under guard; later Sultan, 1316-1320.
- **Habits/Mannerisms:**
  - Pigeons (invented).
  - Music.
  - Talks to guards as if they were friends.
- **Background:** son of Alauddin, born about 1299.
- **Internal Conflicts:** none he shows.
- **External Conflicts:** Kafur.
- **Notes:**
  - **Record:**
    - Kafur sent men to blind or kill him; he turned them, and Alauddin's paiks killed Kafur and freed him.
    - He became regent, then Sultan from 14 April 1316.
    - He dispersed the paiks who had freed him.
    - In 1318 he had the blind princes at Gwalior killed.
    - He was murdered on 9 July 1320 by his favourite, Khusrau Khan.

### Iqbal

*Fifteen • Kafur's rooms at Siri*

- **Role in Story:**
  - The scribe who takes down the book.
  - He writes the coda: the night the paiks came.
- **Goal:** to survive being useful to a dangerous man.
- **Physical Description:** ink on his fingers, a clean hand, and a reed that shakes when the dictation turns bad.
- **Personality:** frightened, honest and observant. He asks one question he should not (chapter 13).
- **Occupation:** a clerk from the palace school.
- **Background:** knows nobody at court, which is why Kafur chose him.
- **Internal Conflicts:** loyalty to the voice against the danger of the pages.
- **External Conflicts:** anyone who finds out what he is writing.
- **Notes:** **Invented.** He is the book's only invented principal.
